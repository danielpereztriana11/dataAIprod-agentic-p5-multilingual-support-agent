**Agent:** P5 — multilingual support agent for an ed-tech LMS (universities and corporate academies, 14 countries, support in 6 languages, 20 agents). It answers in the customer's language from an English knowledge base and routes sensitive requests (grades, accessibility, data access) to a native-speaking agent. Trust level: L3 per language, with a per-language switch that can drop it to L2.

## Session 1 — the six questions

| 1 | **EU AI Act:** which risk tier is our agent in, and why? | **Limited risk — transparency obligation (Art. 50):** a chatbot that talks to learners, instructors and administrators must tell them they are talking to an AI. It is **not high-risk**, *on one condition*: Annex III (point 3) lists as high-risk the AI systems used in education to evaluate learning outcomes, decide on access or admission, or assess a student's level. Our agent stays out of that list only because it **never** decides or comments on grades, assessments, admissions or certificates — those requests are routed to a human (see row 4). If the scope ever drifts into "explain why I got this grade", we must reassess the tier. AI literacy duty (Art. 4) applies to the support team that supervises it. | Legal / DPO — confirm the Annex III reading; confirm the application dates of Art. 50 for our launch date. |
| 2 | **Data:** whose data does it use, and on what legal basis? | Three kinds of data: **(a)** the English knowledge base — ours, no personal data; **(b)** ticket content written by learners, instructors and admins of our **customer institutions**, who are the data **controllers** — we act as **processor** (GDPR Art. 28) under the data-processing agreement of the LMS contract, for the purpose "providing support for the service"; **(c)** accessibility requests can reveal a disability, i.e. **health data (Art. 9, special category)** — the agent must detect and route them, never store them in prompts or eval-sets. The LLM provider is a **sub-processor**: it must be listed in our DPAs, with EU data residency or Standard Contractual Clauses, and no training on our data. Using past tickets to build eval-sets needs the controllers' authorisation or full anonymisation. | DPO — DPA wording with customers; sub-processor list; whether any customer has learners under 18. |
| 3 | **Accountability:** who is accountable when it is wrong — and where is the human? | The **company** is liable for what the agent says, as for any employee (*Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024). **Operational owner: Head of Support.** **Launch decision per language: Product Manager**, on the evidence of that language's eval. **Where the human is:** (1) every sensitive or low-confidence ticket goes to a **native-speaking agent**; (2) L3 = the agent answers routine tickets alone, and a native speaker **reviews a sample** of answers each week in each language; (3) the **per-language switch** (L3 → L2, the agent only drafts and a human approves) is held by the Head of Support and can be flipped without a deployment. | Team — name the people; agree the sample size per language in S5. |
| 4 | **Never:** what must it never do? Also: never answer without a cited source from the approved English knowledge base (no source → hand-off), and never show one institution's data to another. | Test in S4: adversarial sensitive tickets in all 6 languages; target 100 % routed. |

We monitor first-response time **of escalated tickets, per language**. | S5 — define the counter-metric and the alert threshold per language. |

## Session 2 — the cost of error
- **Which cost-of-error line is a legal or reputational risk, not just money?** The **sensitive-requests** line: a wrong or automated answer on a grade, an accessibility accommodation or a data-access request is a **GDPR risk** (health data under Art. 9; a missed one-month deadline for an access request under Art. 15), a breach of the contract with the institution, and a **reputational** risk with universities, who buy on trust. This is why the category must stay **out of scope (Include = 0 in `02_sizing.xlsx`)**: the agent detects it and routes it, it never answers it. Second line: a **silent quality drop in a small language** (Polish, Arabic) hidden by a good average — Simpson's paradox — is a reputational risk in that country before it is a cost.
- **Who in the company owns that risk?** The **DPO** owns the data-access and accessibility line (GDPR); the **Head of Support** owns the routing of sensitive tickets and the per-language switch; the **Product Manager** owns per-language quality and decides to open or close a language.
